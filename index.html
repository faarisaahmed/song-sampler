Click the keys or use your keyboard (A W S E D F T G Y H U J K). Every run of presses starts o → i. After that, Wild keeps it unpredictable and Classic loops o → i → i → a. Pausing for a second starts again at o.

How it works

Syllables. Notes played together count as one syllable. The song is cut into passages at rests, and every passage starts with o i and ends with a.

Wild fills in the middle of each passage using how the real meme flows: from strings like oiiaioiiiai it learns which letter tends to follow which (after a comes i or o, a always follows an i, and so on) and makes up new passages that follow the same patterns. The generator plans ahead so every passage is guaranteed to finish on an a, and long notes lean toward the drawn-out "aaa". The randomness is seeded from the song itself, so it sounds the same every time you play it. 🎲 Reroll gives you a new take.

Classic uses tidy words instead: oia (3 notes), oiia (4), oiiia (5), split so each word's "a" lands on a long note.

Pitch. The three sounds come from the original OIIA cat clip. Each note is made with PSOLA pitch shifting, which rebuilds the sound one vocal cycle at a time. Pitch, length and vowel sound are all set separately, so low notes don't slow down or get muddy and high notes don't turn into squeaky chipmunks. Every note is also matched to the same loudness.

Length. Held stretches each syllable to fill the whole MIDI note. Normal has the cat say each syllable at its natural speed (never longer than the note), so it sounds choppy like the original meme.

Song mode. Search results come from the iTunes Search API (30-second previews). The UVR-MDX-NET Voc_FT model runs in your browser with onnxruntime-web (WebGPU if your browser has it, WebAssembly otherwise) and splits the song into vocals and instrumental. The vocal track then goes through pitch tracking: its pitch is measured every 10 ms, and the curve is cut into notes wherever the singer breathes, changes pitch, or starts a new syllable. Those notes are sung by the cat over the instrumental. You can bring the original vocals back in with the mixer to compare.

Octave range. Every note gets moved up or down by whole octaves until it fits inside the range. The range is centered on the cat's own voice (about D#4), where the samples sound the most natural. Deep notes move up and very high notes move down.
