Click the keys or use your keyboard (A W S E D F T G Y H U J K). Your presses go o → i → i → a over and over, and pausing for a second starts again at o.

How it works

Syllables. Notes played together count as one syllable. The song is cut into passages at rests, and each passage is sung as words that always start with o i and end with a: oia (3 notes), oiia (4), oiiia (5). Every length from 3 up can be built from those. When there's a choice, the split that puts the drawn-out "aaa" on long notes and right before breaks wins, so the same song always gets the same lyrics.

Pitch. The three sounds come from the original OIIA cat clip. Each note is made with PSOLA pitch shifting, which rebuilds the sound one vocal cycle at a time. Pitch, length and vowel sound are all set separately, so low notes don't slow down or get muddy and high notes don't turn into squeaky chipmunks. Every note is also matched to the same loudness.

Length. Held stretches each syllable to fill the whole MIDI note. Normal has the cat say each syllable at its natural speed (never longer than the note), so it sounds choppy like the original meme.

Octave range. Every note gets moved up or down by whole octaves until it fits inside the range. The range is centered on the cat's own voice (about D#4), where the samples sound the most natural. Deep notes move up and very high notes move down.
